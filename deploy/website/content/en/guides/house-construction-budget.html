How to budget your house build and keep payments under control

Most house builds go over budget in the same places: finishes chosen late, advances never adjusted, and small cash payments nobody added up. A budget by phase and a few payment rules prevent most of it.

Build the budget by phase

Split the budget the way the work happens, then split each phase into its main items.

PhaseMain items
Design and approvalsArchitect, structural engineer, approval fees, soil test
Grey structureExcavation, concrete, steel, blocks, plaster, labour, water
FinishingElectrical, plumbing, tiles, stone, woodwork, kitchen, aluminium, paint, fixtures
HandoverSnag repairs, cleaning, utility connections
ContingencyFor the unexpected, commonly about 10% of the total

Decide the finishes early

Finishing is where budgets slip, because choices are made late and one at a time. Choose the tiles, sanitary ware, kitchen and lights before the grey structure ends, price them, and put them in the budget.

Rules for paying

  • Pay for work done and checked, not for time passed.
  • Keep advances small, and adjust them against the following bills.
  • Keep proof of every payment: a bill, a receipt or a transfer slip.
  • Hold back a small retention, often around 5%, until the snag list is cleared.
  • Give petty cash in small amounts, against receipts, and top it up weekly.
  • Compare what you have paid each supplier with what they delivered.

Watch the cash flow

Money is needed in waves: large payments for concrete and steel during the grey structure, then for tiles, woodwork and fittings during finishing. A month-by-month cash forecast shows when you need funds, so work doesn't stop while you wait for money.

Questions people ask

How much contingency should I keep when building a house?

Many owners keep about 10% of the total budget for the unexpected, and more if the design is not final.

Should I pay my contractor in advance?

A small mobilisation advance is common. Keep it small and adjust it against the following bills, so payments always follow work done.

What is retention in a construction contract?

A small part of each payment, often around 5%, held back until the snag list is cleared and the work is handed over.
