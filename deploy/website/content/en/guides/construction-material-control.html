How to stop material going missing on a building site

On most house builds, material is a large part of the cost, and it is where money quietly leaks: short deliveries, extra bags used, items that leave the site. You don't need a warehouse system to stop it, just a routine that every delivery and every release follows.

Where material is lost

  • Short deliveries: 95 bags on a 100-bag delivery note, or steel lighter than billed.
  • Wrong quality: a cheaper brand or a thinner bar than ordered.
  • No record of use: bags released without anyone noting where they went.
  • Waste: mortar mixed and left to set, broken blocks, badly cut tiles.
  • Theft: material leaving the site after hours.

The five-step routine

1. Order in writing

Every purchase starts with a written order: item, brand, size, quantity and rate. A delivery that doesn't match an order is questioned at the gate.

2. Receive at the gate

Count the bags, count or weigh the steel, and check the brand and size against the order. Photograph the supplier's delivery note and the load, and note who received it.

3. Keep a proper store

Keep cement dry and off the floor, in stacks no more than about 10 bags high, and use the oldest bags first. Keep fittings and small items locked. Make one person responsible for the store.

4. Release to a named person for a named job

Material leaves the store only to a named mistri or foreman, for a named piece of work: '20 bags to Akram for the first-floor columns'. Returns are recorded the same way.

5. Reconcile every week

Compare what was issued with what the work needed. For example, a 1:2:4 concrete mix uses roughly 6 to 7 bags of cement per cubic metre; your engineer can tell you what a pour should need. Count the store by surprise every few weeks.

Water tankers too

Where there is no mains water, water is bought by the tanker. Record each tanker's size, time and who received it, and check the tank levels, so you pay only for water that arrived.

Questions people ask

How high can cement bags be stacked?

Keep stacks to about 10 bags high, on a dry raised platform away from the walls, and use the oldest bags first.

How do I check a sand or crush delivery?

Measure the truck's loaded length, width and height, multiply them, and compare the volume with what the supplier is charging for.

Who should keep the site store?

One trusted person, usually the store keeper or supervisor. Material should leave the store only through them, to a named person for a named job.
