Roof slab casting checklist: what to check before, during and after the pour

A roof slab is poured in a day and lasts for decades. Most slab problems, such as cracks, sagging, exposed steel and leaks, come from something that could have been checked before the concrete arrived. Go through this list with your engineer.

Before the pour: formwork

  • Shuttering level and at the right height; props on firm ground, braced and evenly spaced
  • Joints tight, so cement slurry doesn't leak out
  • Formwork clean and oiled, with no wood chips, wire or standing water
  • Openings for stairs, ducts and skylights in the right place

Before the pour: steel

  • Bar sizes and spacing as in the structural drawing
  • Laps as long as the drawing specifies, and staggered
  • Cover blocks under the bottom bars and at the edges, at the cover in the drawing (often about 20 to 25 mm for slabs)
  • Top bars held up on chairs so they are not pressed down
  • Steel clean: no mud, oil or loose rust
  • Beam and column steel tied in as drawn

Before the pour: services

  • Electrical conduits and boxes fixed, joints taped
  • Sleeves for plumbing and drainage through the slab
  • Fan hooks and anchor points in place
  • Everything photographed, because it will soon be covered

During the pour

  • The mix as specified (for example 1:2:4, or a ready-mix grade), with no extra water added to make it flow
  • Concrete vibrated, especially around the steel and at beam joints
  • Poured continuously, without long stops that leave cold joints
  • Thickness checked across the slab
  • Test cubes taken, if your engineer asks for them

After the pour: curing

  • Once the surface has set, keep the slab wet for at least 7 days, longer in hot, dry weather
  • Make small mortar ponds and keep them filled, or cover the slab with wet hessian
  • No heavy loads or stacked material on the new slab
  • Leave the props until your engineer says they can be removed

Questions people ask

How long should a roof slab be cured?

Keep it wet for at least 7 days after it sets, and longer in hot, dry weather. Ponding with small mortar bunds is the usual method on house sites.

When can the props under a slab be removed?

Only when your structural engineer allows it. It depends on the span, the concrete and the weather; for house slabs it is often around two weeks.

Can water be added to concrete to make it easier to pour?

No. Extra water weakens the concrete. If the mix is too stiff, ask your engineer; a well-designed mix is workable without it.
