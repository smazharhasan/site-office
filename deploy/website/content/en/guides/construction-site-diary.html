How to keep a construction site diary that settles arguments

A site diary is the daily record of what happened on site. Good engineers have always kept one, because when there is a dispute about a delay, a payment or a defect, the diary is the evidence. It takes five minutes a day.

What to record every day

  • Date, weather and working hours, including rain or heat that stopped work
  • Who was on site: mistris and labour by trade, and any visitors
  • Work done, by location: 'first-floor slab steel fixed, east side'
  • Material received and released
  • Instructions given, and by whom, especially changes from the owner or architect
  • Inspections and tests, and their result
  • Problems: delays, accidents, shortages and defects
  • Photos of each of the above

Why it matters

  • Payments: claims for work done are backed by a dated record.
  • Delays: you can show which days were lost to rain, approvals or late material.
  • Quality: years later you can see when something was built and who was there.
  • Changes: spoken instructions become written ones.

Tips for a diary people actually keep

  • Make one person responsible, usually the supervisor.
  • Fill it in on site during the day, not from memory at night.
  • Prefer photos to long text: one photo with a caption beats a paragraph.
  • Let the owner and the builder read the same diary.
  • Never change old entries; add a correction instead.

Questions people ask

Who should keep the site diary?

The site supervisor or engineer, every day. The owner and the builder should both be able to read it.

Is a WhatsApp group enough as a site diary?

It helps, but messages get lost, are not organised by date or place, and can be deleted. A diary keeps the same facts in order, in one place.
